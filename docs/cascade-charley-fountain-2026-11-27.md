# Cascade Charley dry fountain

The University of Oregon exhibit dates Cascade Charley to 1991 and credits artist Alice Wingwall. The state public art catalog describes its upper and lower basins and materials including concrete, granite and glazed tile. The model represents the dry lower basin observed in February 2026. The 2014 photo provides a second view of the stepped walls, central outlet, pool rim and nearby rock and brick paving. Source imagery is kept in the research files. The mesh, vertex colors and tile pattern were generated for this project. [University exhibit](https://expo.uoregon.edu/spotlight/history-uo-architecture/feature/cascade-charley) · [Public Art Archive](https://publicartarchive.org/art/Cascade-Charley/cc0e093c) · [2014 photo, Another Believer](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cascade_Charley,_University_of_Oregon_(2014).JPG) · [February 2026 field observations](https://blogs.uoregon.edu/geologyofcampus/2026/02/26/cascade-charley-oregon-geology/).

Four county orthophotos constrain the pool placement. The historical NOAA lidar window gives 37 low returns inside the basin with a median elevation of 2.683 m in the scene frame. Fourteen points on or near the north coping give a 2.858 m median. The east coping check gives a 2.848 m median from six returns. The fitted lower pool has an 18.47 m² outer footprint, a 14.21 m² opening and a 0.239 m floor-to-coping difference. The photos and the reported old waterline help interpret the lidar returns. The exact water state during the historical scan is unresolved. [Aerial registration](cascade-charley-science-walk-screen-2026-11-27.md) · [NOAA lidar tile](https://noaa-nos-coastal-lidar-pds.s3.amazonaws.com/laz/geoid18/8866/44123A1310.copc.laz) · [Editable coordinates and source hashes](../data/metadata/cascade-charley-fountain.json).

The local model includes the stepped concrete masses, dark cap and open outlet, low coping, blue lining with a visible repair patch, and eight representative rocks in the basin and along the brick-colored apron. The rear surface bands rise toward the east. Wall profiles, stone shapes, tile dimensions and the apron pattern are estimates. The upper water path, exact rock mosaic, sign text and listening markers still need finer registration. Water flow and its operating schedule are outside this dry-state model.

The geometry extends the courtyard south package. Three collision envelopes cover the pool, rear walls and rock apron. The two stair handrail envelopes are retained. The basin and walls do not act as walking surfaces. The southern pavement and walk paths retain their original geometry. The northern package retains the same mesh and adopts the updated south descriptor hash. ## Verification

Desktop and mobile production previews match 611 registered exterior IDs, 404 successful resource responses and 83 file hashes. Browser rays hit the lower tile surface at 2.647 m, the north rim at 2.882 m and the high east concrete at 6.820 m. The pool, rim, high wall and rock apron are blocked, and the tested approach positions are clear. The seven sampled routes match their prior geometry, stay within 0.13 m of registered paving and have at least 0.680 m of centerline clearance. The standing surface and collision agree at all 1,467 dense route samples.

The eight focused browser cases, 200 unit tests, production build, 527 archived-file checks and viewer hash validation pass. The south and north GLBs and descriptors and the compiled routing coverage reproduce byte for byte. The change is local to the research worktree.

The NOAA tile header lists a 2017 file creation date. NOAA catalogs the underlying Lane County collection as 2013–2015. [NOAA acquisition record](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/57407).
