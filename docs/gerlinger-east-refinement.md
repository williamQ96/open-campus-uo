# Gerlinger Hall east facade refinement

2026-11-03; local and unpublished. Building `roof-9ac93ab7`, planting `ENV-03`.

UO's [2016 historic assessment](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/gerlinger_hall_historic_assessment_2016-01-20.pdf), pages 4 and 7, identifies the five-bay pedimented entrance as the east elevation. Its floor plan shows the short end of the long east–west hall. The current source-mesh footprint's eastern end is approximately east -359 m, north -198 to -222 m; runtime ray samples at selected heights show a faceted eastern wall from about -359.1 to -360 m. This source, geographic frame and mesh evidence resolve the previous north/east placement error; the historical roof footprint and image alignment still are approximate, not measured field control.

A new independently pinned layer adds a pale triangular pediment, simplified five-bay sash pattern, burgundy awnings, central entrance, two shallow balcony rails and a pair of decorative shrubs. Ground placement samples the existing heightfield. The design interprets the archived [2013 photograph](../data/reference-images/photos/gerlinger-hall/exterior-2013.jpg) by Rick Obst, [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), and UO's link-only official assessment; see [image metadata](../data/reference-images/manifest.json). No reference pixels are embedded. Dimensions, exact proportions, hidden sides, accessibility, shrub species and 2026 condition remain unverified. The original GLB and stable ID are unchanged; the earlier north-facing Hall candidate remains disabled and retained. This is an increment, not an approved level-2 standard or promotion; Hall and ENV-03 remain level 1.

[Before](images/hall-east-before.png) and [after](images/hall-east-after.png) are the same controlled browser view with existing procedural trees hidden for legibility; [the context view](images/hall-east-after-context.png) retains the original trees. These are diagnostic renders, not photographs. The added geometry groups materials by building or vegetation with vertex colors to lower draw calls, retaining the independent building identity and existing toon/PBR support. The existing Johnson and Annex layers remain active. The source-pinned rebuild guard includes the new layer.

Validation: local production build, 49 JS tests, eight rebuild-protection tests, and browser checks for Annex selection, Hall/landmark search, material modes and the existing draw-call ceiling; an initial ceiling failure was resolved by batching. Visual reviews use Chrome/SwiftShader and are not hardware performance measurements. No interior or terrain is modified.

## East masonry detail study, 2026-11-10

The archived Rick Obst 2013 east photograph (`gerlinger-hall-2013`, CC BY 4.0) was visually inspected. It shows alternating projecting brickwork along the central section and light wedge-like window crowns above the burgundy awnings. A separate source-pinned `gerlinger-east-masonry-detail.json` interprets these with two restrained alternating brick strips (36 blocks) and five three-piece light crowns. All 51 pieces merge into one vertex-colored building mesh. The existing east overlay, portico, doors, windows, awnings, roofs, plants, base GLB, collision and navigation remain unchanged; this is still level 1.

The source model's irregular wall is not a measured facade. Initial outer-corner candidates were rejected after visual review revealed that extrapolating the window-alignment coordinates placed blocks off the sloping outer wall. Only the two central strips were retained, with horizontal intersection sampling of the actual GLB triangles at their positions establishing source wall x coordinates near -359.26 and -359.782353. The retained shallow blocks straddle these wall surfaces instead of floating beyond them. Their spacing and number remain artistic approximations, not a brick survey. Outer corner quoins are deliberately deferred.

Existing awnings end at height 17.683 and the upper sash starts at 18.033 in the current model. The new crowns are deliberately compressed to 0.20 m tall in this gap; their rotated bounds are tested to avoid both awnings and sash. They do not reproduce full photographic proportions, carved details or current conditions. No photo pixels are embedded and reference licensing is unchanged.

Validation: `tests/gerlinger-masonry.test.js` checks the source/presentation digest, additive flag, component counts, crown clearance including rotation, merged finite geometry and wrong-source rejection. `tests/browser/gerlinger-masonry.spec.js` checks actual restored player coordinates, first-person camera direction, Gerlinger selection, entrance records, collision probes and draw budget. Final front and oblique comparisons passed: draws 64 to 65 and 44 to 45, both viewpoints collision-clear, unchanged entrance records and no page errors. All four `docs/images/gerlinger-masonry-{front,oblique}-{before,after}-2026-11-10.png` images were inspected; the final central strips sit against the wall and the restrained crowns do not obscure the retained windows or awnings. This is a limited software-rendered visual/model-consistency check, not a field survey or accessibility claim. Detailed browser run artifacts were placed outside the repository to avoid Vite reload interference. Local only, not deployed.
