# Fenton Hall exterior refinement

## Orientation correction and multi-elevation follow-up, 2026-11-04

The earlier layer and its documentation incorrectly interpreted the published principal-entrance photograph as south-facing. The [Oregon state historic archive's Fenton exterior masonry assessment](https://heritagedata.prd.state.or.us/historic/index.cfm?do=main.loadFile&load=56718.pdf), PDF pages 6 and 10, identifies the principal entrance as east-facing and the south elevation as the original eastern building, central tower and western five-story stacks. The [UO 2025 survey](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/fentonhall2025) documents the west stacks, an east-facing entrance photo, historic south-entry alterations and original entrance balustrade. Its site plan and dated images are historical references; neither document is redistributed or used as a texture. The local archive reference SHA-256 is `57fcf64a16a117c9cc38297c0ace4ad098a1ed62d2bdec5387a2d7db11527ee1`.

The reversible overlay relocates the earlier schematic principal entrance from the south to the current model's east projection; its prior entry steps are removed because the current east terrain would bury them, and an illustrative roof balustrade is added. It also adds schematic five-story window rhythms and parapet edges to the south and west sides of the stacks. Backing panels conceal generic windows; they are not measured wall construction. The original campus mesh, footprint and existing shrubs remain. [East before](images/fenton-east-before-orientation.png) / [after](images/fenton-east-after-orientation.png), [south before](images/fenton-south-before-orientation.png) / [after](images/fenton-south-after-orientation.png), [west](images/fenton-west-after-orientation.png), [north](images/fenton-north-after-orientation.png).

A limited scene ray sample located the existing east projection; nearby heightfield samples were approximately 4.24–4.38 m. This does not establish a surveyed entry grade. South shrubs remain at their prior illustrative positions, not a verified planting survey. Approximate facade envelopes do not intersect existing navigation graph edges with a 0.5 m margin, but are not added to collision; the existing footprint remains the only building collision. The east steps, accessible approach, 2011-or-later exterior changes, current fenestration, true original-versus-stacks massing and roof details remain unverified. The model still uses a single simplified base building height and stays level 1; no interior is modified. The prior incorrect overlay is preserved for audit at `assets/exterior-refinement/fenton-before-orientation-2026-11-04.json`.

## East entrance and balustrade connection, 2026-11-04

The [UO 2025 survey](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/fentonhall2025), PDF page 4, shows the east entrance as a rectangular door within a decorative arch and a balustrade over the projecting entry. The [Oregon state masonry assessment](https://heritagedata.prd.state.or.us/historic/index.cfm?do=main.loadFile&load=56718.pdf), PDF page 6, confirms the east orientation. These historical references are linked, not redistributed as runtime textures; their photographs do not establish exact 2026 dimensions or current condition.

The previous oversized dark arched opening is now a brick-colored inset with a stone arch surround and a framed rectangular door; a support joins the existing entry cornice to the previously floating balustrade. The original entrance position, existing vegetation, base GLB, collision and interiors remain unchanged. The photograph shows a broad flight of steps, but sampled local terrain is nearly level with the existing model threshold; the flight was not added without resolving the vertical discrepancy. This is not an accessible or verified entry route. The hipped original roof, five-storey stacks and remaining facades still use the existing approximate base geometry; precise massing, fenestration, height and post-2007 conditions remain uncertain. The building remains level 1.

[East, oblique and south scene comparison](images/fenton-entry-connected-context-2026-11-04.png) shows before on the left and after on the right; the south camera is close to an existing wall. The three software-rendered views retained 54, 77 and 36 draw calls. A geometric regression checks support-to-rail/cornice contact and the door face while preserving other objects. Browser checks compared selection, entrance records, two sampled collision positions and the unchanged toon/PBR budget; 153 JS tests and production build passed. This does not establish a comprehensive clearance, accessibility, field measurement or device frame-time benchmark.

The [previous specification](../assets/exterior-refinement/fenton-before-entry-connection-2026-11-04.json) is retained and source/output hashes are synchronized. The [standalone overlay GLB](../assets/exterior-refinement/fenton-entry-connected-overlay-2026-11-04.glb), SHA-256 `6bc5b6ded586f5cbd3c7ba29f02d5969b7458f238b8465b70b9c393e11493572`, contains the Fenton detail and three illustrative shrubs, not the base building, terrain, collision or navigation. Its positions, normals and colors matched after reimport; it is merged geometry and not a new Blender snapshot. It must not be double-loaded with the runtime overlay. Reproduce with `apps/web-preview/scripts/export-fenton-exterior.mjs` to a new output path. Local only, unpublished.

## East elevation two-storey candidate deferred, 2026-11-04

The [UO survey](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/fentonhall2025), page 1, identifies the original east portion as two storeys and the west stacks as five. Its page 4 east photograph depicts two primary window levels, with arched lower openings, in contrast to the current generic three-row east massing. The historical photograph is reference-only and is not redistributed.

An isolated additive candidate explored two east side panels with arched lower and rectangular upper windows. After lowering its initially over-high eaves, the [east and oblique comparisons](images/fenton-east-two-story-study-2026-11-04.png) still show exposed original generic windows between the panels and the central entrance and discontinuous roof-edge bands. The existing estimated footprint has angled, merged surfaces and does not support a coherent correction through these simple rectangular panels. The candidate was **not integrated**; the runtime layer, building identity, collision, navigation and level remain unchanged. A source-matched wall replacement or bounded source geometry edit and further multi-angle correspondence are required before attempting the full elevation; the roof height, true window positions and present conditions remain unverified. The candidate JSON and working screenshots are retained in the parent task workspace, not shipped as assets.

The isolated browser screen rendered before and after at the same camera positions with 54 east and 77 oblique draw calls. This was a visual candidate screen, not a completed navigation, accessibility or device performance validation. No independent GLB or Blender snapshot was produced for the rejected candidate. Local only; unpublished.

## Source triangle filtering experiment, 2026-11-04

The pinned source GLB contains one Fenton mesh with 2,448 positions and 1,244 indexed triangles; the sampled east walls and their generic windows share that mesh. An isolated browser-only experiment cloned the mesh and removed 144 selected source triangles associated with modeled east window/return groups, then displayed a separate schematic two-storey facade candidate. A runtime probe confirmed that the filter executed, and [east and oblique comparisons](images/fenton-source-filter-study-2026-11-04.png) show that overlapping windows on the selected surfaces disappear. The original source GLB, runtime code and current Fenton overlay were not changed. The views retained 54 and 77 draw calls.

This is not a safe integration yet: the source is pinned, but the prototype uses hard-coded triangle offsets without the necessary per-triangle geometry validation, rejection tests or rebuild contract. Adjacent angled end faces still show three generic window levels, and the model's approximate roof and eave do not establish the actual building's proportions. Selection, navigation and collision were not validated for the prototype. The task workspace retains `fenton-source-filter-candidate.json` and `fenton-source-filter-study.spec.js`; the latter intercepts the development module only in a local browser test, not in production. No new GLB or Blender snapshot was produced. Keep level 1 and the current runtime.

## Guarded east-window correction, 2026-11-04

The earlier experiment is superseded by an integrated, source-pinned correction. The [UO survey](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/fentonhall2025) records two storeys for the original east portion and shows arched lower openings in its historical east photograph. The current overlay adds an approximate two-level window rhythm on the east wing faces; it does not embed the photo. The previous rectangular backing panels and their discontinuous eaves are not used. The existing source eave and roof remain.

Exactly 144 triangles belonging to twelve generic window layers on selected east and return faces are removed from a cloned runtime mesh. Each triangle's original three indices and every one of its 288 referenced vertices' position, normal and color components must match the pinned source before any mutation. The actual downloaded GLB bytes are SHA-256 checked before parsing; source identity, mesh uniqueness, topology, duplicate entries and coverage are also checked. The 1,100 remaining triangles and all vertex attributes are retained; source GLB bytes, building selection identity, collision and interior are unchanged. A mismatched patch fails closed. The rebuild guard rejects a changed GLB rather than reusing the layer. [East and oblique before/after](images/fenton-source-guarded-comparison-2026-11-04.png); [previous specification for rollback](../assets/exterior-refinement/fenton-before-source-filter-2026-11-04.json).

The angled end faces still retain generic three-level windows, and the entrance steps, accessible route, precise windows, material, roof and true two-storey versus five-storey massing remain unverified. This is a partial approximation, not a surveyed reconstruction or a verified level 2; Fenton remains level 1. Browser checks compared selection, entrance records, two sampled collision positions and draw calls before/after; the existing toon/PBR draw budget and the Overview A/B walking navigation regression passed. Unit tests validated the actual source SHA-256, every selected index and vertex attribute, unchanged other indices, and rejection without mutation on changed source hash, vertex, index, duplicate signature and ambiguous target. 156 JavaScript tests, 23 Python tests, build and formatting checks passed. A browser test also changes one GLB byte and verifies that loading is rejected before parsing. The build retains its existing large-chunk warning. These checks are not a full collision survey or device frame-time benchmark.

The [standalone additive overlay GLB](../assets/exterior-refinement/fenton-east-source-guarded-overlay-2026-11-04.glb), SHA-256 `2eef0a8a2bc69f133fd0e7ceaeaffa038bde31f45ca8a7aa29e3221e2dbc6efc`, contains the authored facade and three illustrative shrubs. Its positions, normals and colors matched on reimport; it does **not** contain the source building, source-triangle filter, terrain, navigation or collision. Loading it on the unfiltered source alone will reveal the old-window overlap. The JSON and source-patch code are the editable procedural source; no updated Blender snapshot was made, and the overlay must not be double-loaded with the runtime layer. Local only; unpublished.

## Corner lower windows and roof review, 2026-11-04

The [UO Fenton survey](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/fentonhall2025) describes the original east section as two storeys and its south-elevation photograph on page 7 shows low windows near ground level beneath the two main storeys. The photo is a historical reference only and is not redistributed or embedded. On the two existing angled corner faces, the previous generic lowest-level windows are replaced by shorter, horizontally proportioned windows. Their exact location, grade, material and current appearance are inferred from the existing model and historical photo, not measured. The source-pinned runtime filter validates 24 additional old-window triangles and their vertex signatures before cloning the source mesh; it now removes 168 verified source triangles in total. The existing roof, entrance, upper windows, navigation, collision and source GLB are unchanged.

[East, oblique and south comparisons](images/fenton-basement-comparison-2026-11-04.png) show before on the left. The new lower windows are close to the modeled grade and partially obscured in some views; that is not proof of actual foundation or accessibility conditions. The [aerial scene review](images/fenton-roof-aerial-review-2026-11-04.png) shows the present stylized roof. Although the survey calls for a hipped roof, the current single source mesh merges the original two-storey mass with the taller west stacks and has an irregular roof footprint; the available reference does not establish a safe per-triangle roof boundary or measured ridge and eave junctions. The roof remains unchanged pending more precise plan/height evidence. Fenton remains level 1.

The [previous source specification](../assets/exterior-refinement/fenton-before-basement-windows-2026-11-04.json) is retained. The [standalone additive overlay](../assets/exterior-refinement/fenton-basement-overlay-2026-11-04.glb), SHA-256 `6ac0e1a5f4f8031fd281084f3f832850c71ef2b780b6ed231438c87f54a4f581`, passed position, normal, color and material-color export/reimport checks. It excludes the underlying building, source triangle filter, terrain, navigation and collision; using it on an unfiltered base reveals old windows. The current JSON and source-patch code remain the editable source; no new Blender snapshot was made. Do not double-load the overlay with runtime geometry.

In Chrome/SwiftShader on this machine, six in-memory SHA-256 digests of the 34,766,896-byte GLB took 22.5, 22.1, 23.4, 22.0, 23.0 and 22.7 ms. Three fresh-context scene-ready samples with validation were 1,554, 1,409 and 1,426 ms; a local test bypass had 1,559, 1,589 and 1,547 ms. The bypass involved route interception and these small samples do not isolate end-to-end overhead or represent other devices, networks or caches. SHA-256 still processes the complete file and needs a secure browser context.

Three matching browser viewpoints checked camera positions, selection, entrance records, sampled collision and unchanged draw calls. The existing source-tamper rejection, draw-budget and walking-navigation tests passed; 157 JS tests, 23 Python tests, build and formatting checks passed. These are not an exhaustive clearance survey or hardware performance benchmark. Local only; unpublished.
