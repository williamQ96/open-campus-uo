# Generic bicycle drivetrain study — 2026-11-04

The reusable bicycle now has simplified hubs, a right-side chainring, rear sprocket, two chain runs, grips and small brake levers. These are generic stylized forms, not a model of a specific bicycle or proof of UO fleet design. [UO Transportation Services](https://transportation.uoregon.edu/bike) gives campus cycling context, not the geometry or placement of these particular vehicles. No third-party geometry, image or marks were copied. Original generated geometry is CC BY 4.0 and source code Apache-2.0. PROP-03 remains level 1.

[Before](images/bicycle-drivetrain-before.png) / [after](images/bicycle-drivetrain-after.png) show controlled neutral-scene views. The default rider-inclusive envelope, route, collision data and rider attachment points are unchanged. The chain and levers are schematic and do not animate; actual sizes, styles, placements and current conditions remain unverified. Existing car, SUV, van, pickup and scooter geometry, normals and colors were compared byte-for-byte and remain unchanged. The bicycle increased from 4,032 to 5,184 non-indexed vertices, or 384 triangles per visible instance; all parts are merged into the existing single mesh and material.

The procedural JavaScript is authoritative. The regenerated six-kind GLB was compared vertex-by-vertex against all six runtime-generated prop meshes. The new `assets/props/campus-props-bicycle-2026-11-04.blend` is an editable snapshot of that GLB; on reopening, all six imported meshes' vertex coordinates, face counts and object world transforms matched a fresh import. The snapshot records its source GLB SHA-256 and does not independently update runtime code. Previous snapshots, including `campus-props-scooter-2026-11-04.blend`, remain intact.

Validation includes production build, Node unit and rider contact checks, browser export and campus activity/navigation checks, and the existing fewer-than-180-draw-calls check with activity disabled. The nearby model review is not a campus-context close-up, and no hardware benchmark, survey, accessibility assessment or real-world traffic claim is made. No building interior or campus layout changed.
