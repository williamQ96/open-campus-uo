# Knight Library north facade refinement

2026-11-03. Local project work; not published. Building ID `roof-6684e091`.

Knight Library was chosen because three openly reusable photographs are already documented in the project's reference archive, and UO Libraries supplies a detailed account of its historic exterior. This revision retains the existing geographic placement, massing, palette, seven tall windows, three suggested balconies, and the Architectural Toon presentation. It adds distinct paired entrances and upper windows at the end pavilions, layered stone framing, spandrel and cornice detail, and individual blank discipline plaques. It removes the earlier invented single "KNIGHT LIBRARY" inscription across the window plaques.

Sources: [UO Libraries exterior features](https://researchguides.uoregon.edu/historic-knight/exterior-features) describes the parapet, cornice, frieze and discipline plaques; [building history](https://researchguides.uoregon.edu/historic-knight/building) documents the original 1937 building and subsequent additions. The existing reference archive's `knight-north-a-2018`, `knight-north-b-2018`, and `knight-light-2017` entries contain their image sources, credits, licenses, and hashes. No photographs are embedded in the GLB. No restricted GIS was used.

This remains an interpreted exterior asset, not surveyed geometry. The heights, door sizes, offsets, plaque text and carvings, side/rear elevations, roof, and current post-renovation state are unverified. The plaques are deliberately blank until placement and typography are established from suitable elevation evidence; the small ornaments are simplified original geometry, not literal sculptural reproductions. The source building is grouped into a semantic mesh and can be regenerated from `scripts/build-visual-pilot.py`. The standalone blend and GLB retain campus coordinates and the stable building ID; they have no interior or collision mesh.

Validation: Blender 4.5.15 rebuilt the pilot and campus expansion; `scripts/check-campus-expansion.py` passed with 534 unique building identities, 21 context batches and no embedded images. A standalone Blender render was visually reviewed, and all 42 preview unit tests passed. This does not establish browser performance or measured accuracy. Existing image attribution and source manifest remain in force.

## Source-pinned balcony refinement (2026-11-10)

The additive runtime layer `apps/web-preview/public/scene/knight-library-balcony-detail.json` now encloses the three illustrative bronze balcony railings with solid stone parapets, return walls and coping, plus tapered underside supports. The archived `knight-north-a-2018` photograph was visually inspected: solid projecting stone balconies are clearly visible. `knight-north-b-2018` is the companion source. Existing image attribution and licenses remain unchanged; no photographic texture is embedded.

The centers (-571.2, -561.8, -552.4 east meters), base height 5.066 m and north-facade placement are inherited from the existing source asset and checked against its actual balcony/rail vertices. New dimensions are visual approximations, not a measured survey. Rectangular parapets and tapered supports simplify the photo silhouette; relief sculptures, exact curvature, inscriptions and current conditions are not claimed. Original rail geometry remains untouched behind the additive walls. Tall windows, footprint, landscaping, collision and other landmarks are unchanged. Asset level remains 2.

The detail pins the unchanged GLB hash and is itself hash-pinned in `presentation.json`. Existing rendering merges its 21 stone components to one draw mesh; no runtime code change or Blender rebuild was required. `tests/knight-balconies.test.js` verifies the presentation digest, stable centers, additive preservation, finite bounded geometry, merged mesh count and rejection of wrong source hashes or building identities. Browser before/after evidence is produced separately by `tests/browser/knight-balconies.spec.js`.

Browser acceptance: the dedicated before/after test passed in local Chrome/SwiftShader. Entrance records and the sampled approach collision result were unchanged; draw calls increased from 42 to 43, matching the one-mesh budget, with no page errors. `docs/images/knight-balconies-before-2026-11-10.png` and `knight-balconies-after-2026-11-10.png` were visually reviewed: all three parapets align with the retained balconies, the old bronze railings do not visibly protrude in this north close view, and the added lower silhouettes remain below the tall glazing. This is a single-view acceptance, not an exhaustive survey of every angle. The full JS unit suite at this integration point passed 169/169 tests.

## Seven tall-window reveals (2026-11-10)

`knight-library-window-reveals.json` is a second independently hash-pinned additive layer, separate from the balcony refinement. The archived north-oblique photograph shows substantial masonry depth at the sides and heads of the seven reading-room windows. The earlier builder used shallow, stacked frame/glazing planes. New original brick-colored jamb and head volumes have real 1 m modeled depth, with narrow contrasting edge strips. This supplies physical oblique-view occlusion and shaded side surfaces, not a dark rectangle painted across the windows.

The existing seven centers, glazing, pane divisions and blank plaques are retained. The clear opening remains 3.8 m wide while the source pane width is 3.64 m; the new jambs do not cross the original pane aperture. Because the source GLB is intentionally not cut or replaced, the interpretation adds wall thickness outward around the retained pane rather than claiming a surveyed recess. Exact wall thickness, stone/brick detailing and current conditions remain unverified. The archived photo licenses/attribution and level 2 classification are unchanged. All 42 components merge into a single vertex-colored draw mesh. The balcony layer, original GLB, campus placement, entrances and collision remain untouched.

`tests/knight-window-reveals.test.js` checks the exact detail hash, fourteen jambs/seven heads, physical depth, retained clear pane aperture, finite merged geometry and wrong-base rejection. `tests/browser/knight-window-reveals.spec.js` captures front and oblique comparisons with only this new layer excluded in the before state, preserving the completed balconies in both views.

Front/oblique acceptance passed after correcting an initially blocked viewpoint that had restored the default spawn. The test now asserts actual player x/z, first-person mode and south-facing camera direction before capture, and verifies Knight Library selection afterward. Final views use the same coordinates before/after: front (-561.8,158) and oblique (-540,158), both collision-clear. Draws changed 44 to 45 and 41 to 42 respectively, within the one-mesh budget. Entrance records were identical and there were no page errors. The final four `docs/images/knight-reveals-{front,oblique}-{before,after}-2026-11-10.png` images were visually reviewed: the oblique view shows substantial brick side/head surfaces around retained glazing; completed balconies and blank plaques remain unchanged. Existing trees obscure part of the facade in both comparisons and were not moved for the screenshots. This acceptance does not establish surveyed wall thickness or exhaustive visibility from every angle.
