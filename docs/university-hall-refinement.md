# University Hall exterior refinement

The original campus GLB and `roof-9017feee` identity remain intact. A source-pinned replacement approximates the two end towers, sloped roofs, cornice and repeated windows using [Gary Halvorson / Oregon State Archives' 2005 CC BY 4.0 photograph](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Deady_Hall,_University_of_Oregon,_Eugene_-_DPLA_-_1ab3ead15b82b80bb858c0fb7db25bec.jpg) and [UO's 2017 historic assessment](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/17079_uo_deady_hall_assessment_report_final_17_1103_rev_reduced.pdf), including labeled south/east elevations. The photo retains its original license and attribution in the archive; UO's document is linked only. No photographic texture is used.

The tower and mansard geometry, simplified arched window profiles, four illustrative shrubs, dimensions and remaining elevations are simplified and not a current survey. Entries, stairs, access and collision behavior are not verified. The building remains level 1; no interior was changed. ## Four-sided eaves and dormer pediments, 2026-11-04

University Hall was selected for this batch because it already has a source-pinned full exterior with twin towers, two long facades and two ends; Villard still has an incomplete west face and Anstett's other elevations and roof are much less developed. The existing [2005 Gary Halvorson / Oregon State Archives photograph](../data/reference-images/photos/university-hall/exterior-2005.jpg) (CC BY 4.0) shows light dormer pediments, framing and substantial brackets beneath the eaves. The [UO 2025 survey](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/universityhall2025) records the historic building and its restoration; the project also has the UO 2017 assessment referenced above. No reference photograph is embedded in the runtime. The 2005 photo does not establish current details, exact dimensions, or every hidden face; repeats on the opposite sides are simplified interpretation, not a surveyed reconstruction.

The existing exterior's roof dormers have light pediments and side framing, and the main cornice, end elevations and both tower eaves receive continuous trim and stylized brackets. The earlier 210 objects, building position, vegetation and original footprint remain unchanged; 206 new primitives form one coherent eaves and dormer treatment merged into the existing building mesh. The building remains level 1: the mansard and tower roofs, eaves, dormer positions, window counts, ends, entries, terrain and post-restoration condition remain approximate; this batch is not enough evidence to claim Knight-equivalent completion. Existing entry positions and collision were not reauthored or certified accessible. The [2005 Gary Halvorson / Oregon State Archives photograph](../data/reference-images/photos/university-hall/exterior-2005.jpg), CC BY 4.0, shows a tower end with three tall arched openings and a delicate crest; the [UO 2017 assessment](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/17079_uo_deady_hall_assessment_report_final_17_1103_rev_reduced.pdf) identifies symmetrical elevations and the [2025 UO survey](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/universityhall2025) describes mansard towers and cast-iron cresting. The source image is used as reference, not a runtime texture; its date does not establish post-restoration condition.

Both modeled end towers now have three narrow framed arches in place of one broad arch, and simplified dark perimeter cresting on the two existing caps. Opposite-end symmetry, dimensions, grille detail, roof shapes and present-day finish are approximations; the lower facades, existing entrances, grounds, base GLB, navigation, collision and interior are unchanged. This is a focused tower improvement to the existing four-sided approximation, not a completed survey or a new grade. Roof slopes remain simplified hips rather than a fully verified mansard profile; chimney positions, actual dimensions, entrance access and post-restoration details need more evidence. University Hall remains level 1.

## Two-slope mansard study, 2026-11-04

The [UO 2025 survey](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/universityhall2025) describes the mansard roof and towers, and the [2005 Gary Halvorson / Oregon State Archives photograph](../data/reference-images/photos/university-hall/exterior-2005.jpg), CC BY 4.0, shows the tall sloping tower and dormers. The source is used as a reference, not a texture; dimensions and current post-restoration construction cannot be inferred precisely from it.

The earlier constant-slope truncated hip is replaced by a reusable two-slope approximation for the main roof and both towers, retaining their original bounds and anchor. Four tower dormer housings and glazing were adjusted to intersect the revised roof and remain visible. Initial candidate screening revealed buried tower windows and accidentally touched entry objects; the entry objects were restored, and a regression now checks that only the three roofs and four tower dormer pairs differ from the preceding version. Lower facades, entry, surroundings, interior and collision are unchanged. The chosen slope break and dormer housings are illustrative, not a measured roof; chimneys, correct face curvature, roof pitches, all dormers and post-restoration conditions remain uncertain. The building stays level 1.

## East and west entrance stair connections, 2026-11-04

The [UO 2017 historic assessment](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/17079_uo_deady_hall_assessment_report_final_17_1103_rev_reduced.pdf), printed page 40, describes concrete stairs and wing walls at both principal entrances with central metal handrails. The historical document and photograph were consulted only, not redistributed or used as textures. Both existing four-step entries now receive stylized solid sloped concrete wing walls and a center rail with posts. A first isolated candidate used thin sloped bars as walls; an oblique screen showed gaps beneath them, so it was replaced by grounded wedge geometry. The model's existing 480 components, building placement, roof, doors, grounds, collision and interior are retained; 10 objects were added. The reusable `stair-wing` primitive is part of the procedural exterior generator.

The dimensions, grade, original stair detail, actual construction and present-day accessibility remain approximations, not a site survey. Existing terrain samples at the stair sides were about 4.35–4.68 m, and the new wedge bottoms extend below those samples; this is a limited visual fit, not a full terrain or clearance survey. The visual walls and rails have no additional collision boxes, and the existing route and access are not certified. The mansard roof, chimneys and other facets still require further evidence. University Hall remains level 1.

## Restricted stair collision, 2026-11-04

The previous visual-only stair treatment is now supplemented by six narrow 2D collision rectangles: four follow the modeled wing-wall footprints and two cover the center-rail/post envelopes. The existing original building footprint and all visible geometry remain. The approach lanes on either side of the center rail are left open in the model. The [prior visual-only specification](../assets/exterior-refinement/university-before-stair-collision-2026-11-04.json) is retained for rollback.

The project movement check uses a 0.42 m player radius. Simulation moved a player down both side lanes on each staircase, while sampled center and wing positions were blocked. All 7,705 existing navigation edges were screened against the six rectangles with a 0.5 m margin and none intersected. Browser before/after tests retained all entrance records, the University Hall inferred approach, and the complete 38-point Johnson-to-University route; the existing walking regression also passed. This is model consistency, not a surveyed entry, collision-height or accessibility guarantee: the rectangles are full-height 2D approximations and the entry steps do not model vertical traversal. The source is pinned and the prior GLB export still contains only visible geometry, not collision. Level remains 1; no interior changed. Local only; unpublished.
