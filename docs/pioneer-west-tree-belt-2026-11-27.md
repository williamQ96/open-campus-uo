# Pioneer Cemetery western tree belt

The western edge of Pioneer Cemetery contains a continuous line of tall conifers beside the Frohnmayer service drive. The campus GIS query returned 90 current tree records in the wider corridor. Four Lane County aerial dates and the earlier campus atlas support a subset of trunk positions along the belt and in the entrance bed. The 2011 atlas also identifies tree 4432 as an oak. The current GIS leaves its species blank. [Campus Tree App](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/trees) · [Atlas, printed p. 107](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/treeatlas_webversion_07_31_2012reduced.pdf#page=116) · [Atlas, printed p. 120](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/treeatlas_webversion_07_31_2012reduced.pdf#page=129) · [Lane County orthophotos](https://lcmaps.lanecounty.org/arcgis/rest/services/AirPhotos/AirPhotos/MapServer).

The model uses the registered stems of seven Douglas firs, five incense cedars, two western red cedars, a ponderosa pine, a bigleaf maple and two oaks. It also registers four vine maples and represents six low plant groups apparent in the aerials. The NOAA tile supplied 131,620 height returns from the local clip. Its header date is October 31, 2017. Nearby crowns overlap the sampling radius, so the fitted height combines the published inventory dimension and the local upper returns for the main canopy. The entrance oak and smaller stems retain the inventory heights. Crown spreads and calipers use the GIS values. Branching and shrub species are representative. [NOAA point cloud](https://noaa-nos-coastal-lidar-pds.s3.amazonaws.com/laz/geoid18/8866/44123A1309.copc.laz) · [Editable registration](../data/metadata/pioneer-cemetery-west-trees.json).

Sixteen mature trunks register collision. Two cedar trunks in the interior of the belt use visible geometry. The stem position of fir 4444 falls on the Conger lane in the current GIS registration. An earlier map places the fir in that vicinity. Its exact offset remains open, along with the current condition of cherry 4446. The existing cemetery crowns and the Southwest Campus Green grove remain part of the composition. The 2024 street view confirms the southern belt's mix of low growth and higher crowns. [2024 ground view](https://www.google.com/maps/@?api=1&map_action=pano&pano=dyJn7khw4HS8-qqi744JDQ&heading=19.16).

The grass mesh adds 181.98 m² over the remaining gray edge of the inferred shoulder. This strip uses the same turf palette and the pinned Frohnmayer verge grade. The drive, Conger Lane, the parking and the loop sidewalks retain their registered geometry and route data. ## Conger Lane tree check

The atlas symbols for 4444 and 4446 differ from the current GIS points by about 8.6 m and 7.5 m after a coarse registration using 9 nearby controls. The 2017 and 2025 aerial crops show exposed gravel east of the modeled centerline near northing −419 m. Dense crowns hide the continuation around the fir. Within 0.8 m of the recorded fir, 13 of 15 NOAA returns are above 4 m and none fall between 1 m and 4 m. All 17 returns within 0.8 m of the cherry are below 1 m. These checks locate the canopy and an exposed gravel segment. Both stems remain pending ground control. ## Verification

Both production browser profiles match all 611 research model IDs, 394 tracked resources and 71 served-file hashes. Three site mesh layers and 16 trunk collisions load in both profiles. The existing routes through Frohnmayer, the cemetery northwest entry, Cherry–Risdon and Rankin match their prior results. The closest trunk sits 0.954 m from modeled paving and blockers. One hundred triangles of new overhead geometry along sampled paths stay at least 7.657 m above the modeled ground. Five probes confirm the verge replaces the prior visible ground. The two exports reproduce byte for byte. The package is included in the 610-exterior Pages release.
