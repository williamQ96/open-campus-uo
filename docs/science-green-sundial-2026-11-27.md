# Science Green and the Mays sundial

The university's architecture exhibit records the 1912 memorial at Lewis Integrative Science. Contemporary Oregon newspapers describe the London-made gunmetal dial and California granite base. The March 1912 report gives the dial as 22 inches across. The 2026 field group measured 0.55 m for the plate and 0.75 m for the stone width and photographed the base at approximately 1 m high. The chemistry and color observations support a pale, speckled granitic material. [UO exhibit](https://expo.uoregon.edu/spotlight/history-uo-architecture/feature/sundial) · [March 1912 campus newspaper](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/2004260238/1912-03-27/ed-1/seq-1.pdf) · [2026 field observations](https://blogs.uoregon.edu/geologyofcampus/2026/02/26/dont-take-this-sundial-for-granite/).

The 2017, 2021, 2023 and 2025 county aerials show the stone and a distinct shadow on the Science Walk forecourt. Their screen estimates center near scene east −59.97 m and north 75.65 m. The field-report GPS is about 5 m west of this position. A group of 2017 NOAA returns to the northeast reaches 1.13 m above neighboring ground. That helps constrain height. Dimensions, separation from the base and local image registration limit how precisely the lidar points can locate the stone. [Lane County aerials](https://lcmaps.lanecounty.org/arcgis/rest/services/AirPhotos/AirPhotos/MapServer) · [NOAA lidar tile](https://noaa-nos-coastal-lidar-pds.s3.amazonaws.com/laz/geoid18/8866/44123A1310.copc.laz) · [Editable measurements and source hashes](../data/metadata/science-green-sundial.json).

The model includes the irregular stone base, 0.55 m dial face, two engraved-ring outlines, representative hour lines and north-facing sloped gnomon. It uses original mesh geometry. The campus newspaper mentions a historical time-correction plaque in a face of the pedestal. The recent views leave its current condition and lettering unresolved. Gnomon details, fine dial text and rock texture use estimates. [June 1912 photograph and caption](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn83045782/1912-06-16/ed-1/seq-25/ocr/) · [Exporter](../scripts/build-science-green-sundial.py).

The reconstructions include 409.89 m² of pavement around the sundial and along the east side of Science Green, including its connection to the north East 13th sidewalk. The north bed covers 33.55 m² and contains seven representative shrub clusters. Twenty-five new navigation edges follow the photographed approach. The centerline clears the modeled pedestal and north bed by at least 1.228 m. This pass covers the east branch and north forecourt. [UO campus plan, printed p. 144](https://cpfm.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/2023-12/principle-12-design-area-special-conditions_2022_23_.pdf) · [Runtime descriptor](../apps/web-preview/public/scene/science-green-sundial-review/site.json).

The forecourt includes modeled paving joints. Campus inventory records 4385, 4450 and 4451 place a paperbark maple and two columnar white oaks along the approach. Their representative crowns and collidable trunks use the inventory spreads and aerial and lidar height evidence. The four aerial dates show open paving at the previous inferred context tree position. The Science Green overlay removes that tree and restores the underlying tree placement if the overlay cannot load. ## Verification

The two production preview profiles match all 611 exterior IDs, 400 tracked resource responses and 79 sampled file hashes. The two sampled routes measure 98.905 m and 89.204 m, remain on registered paving and clear the nearest solid by 1.314 m and 1.228 m. Four collision envelopes cover the dial and three trunks. Oregon Hall, Education Annex and sampled cemetery routes retain their previous results. The site mesh, descriptor and compiled paving coverage reproduce byte for byte.
